I have been coaching in the CrossFit affiliate space full time since 2014 and have learned some helpful tools to progress and continually challenge myself to become a better coach. Over the past 8+ years, I have worked full time for two different CrossFit affiliates, and have worn many different hats within these gyms, including:

- Head Coach
- Foundations Program Manager
- Kids/Teens Coach
- Nutrition Coach
- Competitors Program Director
- Lesson Plan writer
- Programmer
- … and a few others.

Most recently, I opened my own affiliate in 2022 and took on the responsibility of developing my own coaches.

Over the years, I have had the opportunity to work with some of the best coaches in our space and learned firsthand from them how to become a more effective coach. There are five things, in particular, I have found along the way that have helped me become a better coach. Regardless of experience level, all coaches wanting to commit to excellence can utilize these five tools to hone their skills.

## 1. Learn From Other Coaches (The World’s Greatest Bandits)

There is a saying from a Flowmaster at a Level 1 Seminar: “CrossFit coaches are the best thieves in the world.” There is an abundance of fantastic coaches with particular strengths and specialties, and coaches can learn something from every coach/trainer they encounter.

Coaching cues are a dime a dozen — an effective cue is the one that works. Taking other coaches’ classes, dropping into other affiliates, or using online resources expands the toolkit across teaching, seeing, correcting, presence, attitude, and group management.

## 2. Write Lesson Plans (Failure to Plan is Planning to Fail)

Affiliates that use the exact same warm-up every day can blunt athlete progress and coach effectiveness. A confidently formulated lesson plan improves structure and flow.

Writing lesson plans regularly challenges creativity and engagement. Going into class with a specific plan dials in what to look for and how to get athletes there: scaling options, timeline for each part, stimulus/intended goal. CrossFit’s “Lesson Planning” course is a useful starting point.

## 3. Get a Mentor

Find somebody you respect and admire, and approach them for guidance. Mentorship and feedback-rich environments accelerate coach development. CrossFit EDU’s Coaches Development Program is one structured option for real-time feedback.

## 4. Ongoing Education (You Don’t Know What You Don’t Know)

The Level 1 Seminar is essentially a “learner’s permit.” Build deeper understanding over time. Level 2 focuses on applying L1 with feedback. Levels 3 and 4 are further milestones. Specialty courses, journals, and other coaches’ work continue the journey. Coaching education is never finished.

## 5. Coach! (Do the Thing)

The most effective way to get better at coaching is to coach. High-level coaches accumulate hours on the floor. Practice, accept vulnerability, and treat coaching skill like athletic skill — reps under attention create progress.

## Takeaways for DUCK-WOD Personal Coach

- Keep improving craft continuously (knowledge + practice).
- Plan sessions with clear stimulus, timeline, and scales.
- Learn patterns from strong programming — never copy sessions verbatim.
- L1/L2 remain the foundation; articles like this refine professional posture above that base.
